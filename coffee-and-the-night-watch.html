Why Coffee Became the Drink of Those Who Stand Watch

Coffee, camaraderie, and the generations who work while the world sleeps.

Adela Tortora · Saint Michaels Coffee, LLC · 4 minute read

There is a reason coffee and the night shift seem to belong together.

Long before convenience stores, drive-through coffee shops and energy drinks, people responsible for working through the night found something remarkably familiar waiting for them: a hot cup of coffee.

From police stations and firehouses to military operations, factories, garages and early-morning job sites, coffee became part of the rhythm of working while much of the world slept.

That relationship goes back further than most people realize.

Coffee on the Night Watch

In the late 1800s, American police work looked very different from today.

Officers often patrolled their beats on foot for hours at a time, including overnight shifts through cold streets.

The Cleveland Police Museum preserves an account of what happened when officers returned to Central Station for their midnight meal. In the basement were a stove and table where officers could remove their heavy coats and boots, warm themselves and heat their coffee before returning to their nighttime patrols.

It is a remarkably familiar scene.

Different century. Different uniform.

Same hot cup before heading back out.

America Started Working Around the Clock

As the United States industrialized, more Americans began working schedules that no longer followed daylight.

Factories operated longer hours. Transportation systems ran overnight. Police and fire departments required continuous staffing. Hospitals, utilities and other essential operations never really closed.

Coffee fit naturally into that world.

By the middle of the 20th century, the “coffee break” had become so established in American working culture that it began appearing in formal labor agreements.

In 1951, TIME reported on the growing practice of negotiated coffee breaks for American workers.

What began as a simple pause during the workday had become part of American workplace culture.

There Is Science Behind the Tradition

The connection isn’t purely cultural.

Human beings have a natural circadian rhythm that encourages wakefulness during daylight and sleep at night.

Working against that rhythm can affect alertness, reaction time and concentration.

Caffeine temporarily blocks adenosine receptors in the brain. Adenosine is one of the chemicals involved in building the feeling of sleep pressure throughout the day.

Research involving shift workers has found that caffeine can improve alertness and some aspects of performance during periods when fatigue would otherwise become a problem.

That doesn’t mean coffee replaces sleep.

It doesn’t.

But it helps explain why generations of people working overnight independently reached for the same drink.

More Than Caffeine

There is another reason the coffee pot became part of so many workplaces.

It created a place to gather.

The station kitchen.

The firehouse table.

The factory break room.

The shop.

The garage.

The military operations center.

People poured coffee, exchanged information, talked about what happened on the previous shift and prepared for what came next.

Sometimes important conversations happened around that coffee pot.

Sometimes nothing important was said at all.

That was part of its value too.

For a few minutes, people carrying responsibility could simply sit together before going back to work.

The Tradition Never Really Left

Technology changed.

The jobs changed.

Coffee changed.

But the ritual survived.

There is still a police officer drinking coffee before a night shift.

A firefighter sitting at the station table.

A soldier holding a cup before the day begins.

A truck driver starting another stretch of highway.

A tradesman arriving before sunrise.

A coach unlocking the gym while everyone else is still asleep.

Different jobs. Different responsibilities.

But they share something simple.

Sometimes there is work that simply has to be done.

And for generations, a cup of coffee has been there when that work begins.

At Saint Michaels Coffee, that’s a tradition we understand.

For those who run toward danger, and everyone who stands behind them.
