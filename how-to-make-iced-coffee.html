How to Make Iced Coffee: The Saint Michaels Way

Bold, cold, and never watered down. Make refreshing flash-brewed iced coffee at home in about five minutes.

Saint Michaels Coffee · 5 minute read

Most disappointing iced coffee starts with one innocent mistake: brewing a normal cup of hot coffee and pouring it over a full glass of ice.

The ice melts. The coffee gets weak. The flavor disappears before the glass is cold.

The Saint Michaels way is different. We brew the coffee hot - because hot water extracts the aroma and character we want - but we replace part of the brewing water with a measured amount of ice. The fresh coffee lands directly on that ice, chills quickly, and finishes at the right strength.

It is called flash-brewed iced coffee, and it takes about five minutes.

The Saint Michaels flash-brew recipe

This recipe makes one generous iced coffee, approximately 12 ounces before serving ice.

What you need

  • 25 grams of a fresh medium or medium-dark coffee you enjoy, ground medium-fine
  • 250 grams of hot filtered water
  • 150 grams of ice for brewing
  • Fresh ice for serving
  • A pour-over brewer and paper filter
  • A heat-safe carafe or large mug
  • A kitchen scale, if available

A kitchen scale makes the recipe repeatable: scoops vary with grind size, and a cup of ice varies with cube shape. Treat this ratio as a starting point and adjust to taste.

Step 1: Heat the water

Heat filtered water to approximately 195-205°F. If you do not have a temperature-controlled kettle, bring the water to a boil and let it rest for about 30 seconds.

Water quality matters because coffee is mostly water. If your tap water tastes strongly of chlorine or minerals, filtered water can make a noticeable difference.

Step 2: Rinse the filter

Place a paper filter in the brewer and rinse it with hot water over an empty carafe. Discard all rinse water before adding ice.

Step 3: Measure the ice and coffee

Place 150 grams of ice in the heat-safe carafe beneath the brewer. Add the ground coffee to the rinsed filter and gently level the bed. The brewing ice is part of your total water, not an extra ingredient to estimate by eye.

Step 4: Bloom the grounds

Pour approximately 50 grams of hot water over the grounds, making sure all the coffee is wet. Wait 30-45 seconds.

Fresh coffee releases trapped carbon dioxide when water first reaches it. This short pause - called the bloom - helps the rest of the water move through the grounds more evenly.

Step 5: Finish the pour

Slowly pour the remaining 200 grams of hot water in controlled circles. Avoid pouring directly onto the paper filter. Try to finish the brew in approximately three minutes, although the exact time will vary with the grinder and brewer.

The hot coffee will drip directly onto the ice and chill almost immediately.

Step 6: Swirl and serve

When the dripping stops, remove the brewer and swirl the carafe until the brewing ice has mostly melted.

Fill a glass with fresh ice and pour. Taste it before adding anything. Then make it yours: drink it black, add a splash of milk, or lightly sweeten it.

The result should be fresh and refreshing, with enough strength to hold its own over ice.

Choose a coffee you love

For a rich iced coffee with milk, start with a medium or medium-dark coffee with chocolate or nutty tasting notes. If you prefer a brighter black iced coffee, try a lighter roast. An espresso-style blend can also work in a pour-over; the name does not mean you need an espresso machine.

No pour-over brewer? Use your coffee maker

You can use the same basic principle with a standard drip machine:

  1. Use your normal amount of coffee.
  2. Put roughly 60% of your usual water in the machine.
  3. Place the remaining 40% as measured ice in the carafe.
  4. Brew the hot coffee directly onto the ice.
  5. Swirl, then serve over fresh ice.

Make sure your brewer's manufacturer allows ice in the carafe and that the carafe can safely handle the temperature change. When in doubt, brew into a heat-safe container.

Three fixes for better iced coffee

If it tastes watery

Count the brewing ice as part of the water. Do not brew a full-strength hot recipe and then add an unmeasured glass of melting ice.

If it tastes harsh or bitter

Grind slightly coarser, shorten the brew time, or use slightly cooler water. Change only one variable at a time.

If it tastes sour or thin

Grind slightly finer or extend the brew time. Sourness often means the water did not extract enough flavor from the coffee.

Iced coffee is not cold brew

Iced coffee is brewed hot and then rapidly chilled. Cold brew extracts coffee with cool or room-temperature water over several hours.

Neither is automatically better. Cold brew is often described as round and smooth. Flash-brewed iced coffee usually preserves more of the aroma and lively character associated with hot brewing.

Choose cold brew when you want slow and smooth. Choose the Saint Michaels flash brew when you want bold, fresh, and ready in five minutes.

Make your next glass count

Great iced coffee is not leftover coffee with ice added. It is a recipe designed for ice from the beginning.

Measure the coffee. Measure the hot water. Count the ice as part of the water. Brew it fresh and cool it fast.

No complicated equipment. No waiting overnight. No watered-down cup.

That is iced coffee the Saint Michaels way.
